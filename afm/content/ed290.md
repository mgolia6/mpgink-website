Happy Aloha Thursday!

This week's message comes early, as I head to [upstate New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oneonta,_New_York) for alumni weekend at [Hartwick College](https://www.hartwick.edu/) tomorrow—my first return in 17 years to the woods, the cabins, and the sustainable homes we built with [Sandy Huntington](https://www.buddhistdoor.net/news/buddhist-scholar-c-w-sandy-huntington-dies-aged-71/), the professor who quietly, profoundly changed my life. A little over a year ago I shared [THIS POST](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/simplicity-edition-254-matthew-golia-we2ae/) about my time learning from such an amazing human.

This newsletter was born—more truthfully, inspired—by my time in Sandy's classes. His homework was simple in theory; the practice of completing it was altogether something else. Each week, he gave us seemingly disparate reading assignments and asked us to find the connections between them and write a paper about them. When I started this newsletter in 2018 as a way to connect with a new team, I channeled that same energy—pulling together random facts, national days, births, and deaths, current events and searching for the common thread. Because of that, at least once a week, Sandy is with me—in my heart and in my mind.

I think he'd offer me an encouraging B—recognizing the essence of something special, but nudging me toward refinement, even after 290 editions

Sandy passed away a few years ago after a sudden illness. This weekend, we dedicate one of the homes we built together in his honor. Old friends are returning—some I haven't seen in decades. It's not quite [The Big Chill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Big_Chill_(film)), but it carries that same bittersweet pulse: re-connection, reflection, and the quiet joy of gathering in someone's legacy.

When I learned of Sandy's passing, I wrote the following. I am not a big fan of public speaking, but I think I'll attempt reading it aloud at the dedication. It's a memory, a meditation, and a thank-you. I share it here with you as a way of carrying him forward.

*I breathed each breath knowing that Sandy was somewhere out there in the world. That he was sitting in a classroom, with his slow delivery, expanding minds and hitting home with his teaching. I remember how when things got loud, Sandy got quiet. You almost had to lean in to hear him—and you wanted to hear him.*

*Earth and Breath*

*A restful glance, a squint, and in an instant, I leaned in to hear his words*

*Simple turn of phrase, the turning of each page, engaged in matters quite absurd*

*Architecture of life, of death, razor edge of the knife or breath, and through it all I learned*

*The emptiness of emptiness, that more is more when it is less, the passion with which he served*

*We took earth, clay slurry, the worry of the day, we held it in our grasp*

*Picked apart straw bales, inhaled the summer air, exhaled the past*

*Sun dripped and mud burnt, baptized in Pine Lake at long last*

*Slipped to our cabins, unraveled by the day's work, dreamt long and vast*

*Rising early, more slurry, tiptoed through the forest to find the True tree*

*In the sun's flicker and glow, blessed the forest, palms pressed to prayer, we*

*Carried the wooden body through pine rows, engrossed in the beauty*

*A pattern language emerged, silent, invisible surge, urging us to embrace the holy*

*We meditated with loving kindness, our mind fixed solely on the breath*

*Watching the birth of our thoughts, hurling across, then coaxing our minds to rest*

*Back to the breath, counting to ten, thoughts come again, this time in a full court press*

*But as time passed, the firm grasp, released its clasp, made me feel blessed*

*May I be well, may I be at peace, his words, his voice, cascade through these trees, aloft in the breeze, I recite them to this day*

This weekend isn't just a reunion—it's a return to the source. To the quiet power of a teacher who taught us to build with reverence, to listen with intention, and to live with breath as our guide.

Mahalo Nui, Sandy.
