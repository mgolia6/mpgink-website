# AFM | Little League, Big Dreams Edition | #287

## ☀️ The Illusion of Endless Summer

Happy Aloha Friday! Late August in Connecticut still hums with the warmth of July, even as shadows stretch longer and the air begins to shift. It's the season of denial — when we pretend summer might never end. The light lingers just long enough to fool us, and for the kids at the [Little League World Series](https://www.littleleague.org/world-series/) in [Williamsport, PA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamsport,_Pennsylvania), it's more than a feeling. It's real. They're not just playing baseball — they're holding off the school bell, the early alarms, the return to routine. Every pitch is a protest against the calendar.

## ⚾ Trumbull's Legacy

In 1989, Trumbull Little League stunned the world. A team of Connecticut kids beat Chinese Taipei, rewriting the odds and etching their names into LLWS history. I was eight, watching from just a few towns over, and even then I knew: this was bigger than baseball. It was about possibility. About rewriting your story in real time. That team didn't just win — they gave every kid in the state permission to dream a little bigger.

## 🥲 My Swing and a Miss

A few years later, I stood in my own batter's box. Not a prodigy, not a star — just a kid hoping to belong. I tried out for an all-star team, facing a pitching machine that didn't care about nerves. Twenty pitches. Twenty misses. And then — finally — contact. A shallow hit, maybe two or three total. I knew I wasn't making the team. But it was enough. Enough to remind me that courage isn't about perfection — it's about showing up, even when you're scared. That moment stayed with me far longer than any stat line.

## 🌺 Hawaii vs. Home

After living in Hawaii and falling in love with its baseball culture — Coach Oda's joyful discipline, the island's deep pride — I found myself torn this year. Fairfield, CT was making a run, and early in the tournament, they faced Hawaii. My past and present collided. Who do you root for when both teams carry pieces of your heart? It wasn't just a game — it was a reckoning. A reminder that identity is layered, and sometimes, rooting isn't binary.

## 🔥 Fairfield's Run

Fairfield didn't flinch. They stepped into the spotlight and delivered. They battled through the bracket with grit and grace, winning four straight and earning a spot in the U.S. Championship game this Saturday. Their energy is electric — disciplined but loose, confident but humble. They're not just representing Connecticut; they're channeling every kid who's ever dared to dream from a small town. Their run feels like a revival of Trumbull's spirit, but with a new generation's swagger.

And while Hawaii's 2025 team may not have the firepower of their 2018 or 2022 squads, [Coach Gerald Oda](https://alohastatedaily.com/2025/08/14/q-a-honolulu-little-league-manager-gerald-oda/) reminds us:

*"We don't have to be like how 2018 was or how 2022 was. We just have to make our own beautiful picture together."*

That's the magic of the LLWS. Each team paints its own masterpiece — one pitch, one hug, one affirmation at a time.

## Final Inning

So here we are. The last stretch of August. The final inning of summer. And while most of us are packing up beach chairs, Fairfield is still swinging. Still believing. Still reminding us that magic doesn't follow a schedule. It shows up when you least expect it — when you're down to your last strike, when you're torn between past and present, when you choose to swing anyway.

Here's to the kids who extend summer with every pitch. To the ones who miss twenty times but still swing again. To the hometowns that rally behind them.

And to all of us, standing in our own batter's boxes, hoping for just one clean hit before the season turns.

Mahalo Nui
