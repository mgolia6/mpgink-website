A Beat Writer, a Jazz Revolutionary, and a Civil Disobedient Walk Into a Bar….

EDITION NO. 304 · THURSDAY 3/12/2026 · ALSO ON LINKEDIN ↗
AFM | A Beat Writer, a Jazz Revolutionary, and a Civil Disobedient Walk Into a Bar… | #304

🌺 Happy Aloha Friday,

Cosmic forces altered — or rather, aligned — paths this Aloha Thursday. Sure, tomorrow's back-to-back Friday the 13th celebration would've been ripe for mischief, but with a planned absence on deck, I had two choices: skip a week (cue the boos from the devoted subscribers…LOL) or bump this bad boy up a day. I chose the latter, and in return I was blessed with the kind of kismet only bestowed upon those who pay attention when the universe starts tapping its foot.

A convergence of events, spread over 33 years (1922–1955), all landing on the 12th day of March, quietly tying together three wildly different — yet strangely aligned — humans. So if, by chance, next week at your local bar trivia, someone asks what a Beat writer, a jazz revolutionary, and a civil disobedient have in common…well, read on.

What you're about to read might as well be the opening to some sardonic joke:

Kerouac, Charlie Parker, and Gandhi walk into a Greenwich Village bar…

And somehow, the punchline is history itself.

Three Lives, One Date, One Shared Defiance

Jack Kerouac, born March 12, 1922, spent his life chasing the pulse beneath the pavement — the restless, unfiltered truth of being alive. His writing wasn't polished; it was poured. He broke the rules of literature by refusing to wait for permission to speak in his own rhythm.

Charlie Parker, who died March 12, 1955, did the same with sound. He took the tidy swing structures of the 1930s and detonated them into bebop — fast, angular, improvisational, unapologetically free. Parker didn't just play notes; he carved new space for what music could be.

Mahatma Gandhi, who began the Salt March on March 12, 1930, broke a different kind of structure — the political kind. His 240-mile walk wasn't loud, but it was seismic. A quiet refusal that shook an empire. A reminder that sometimes the most radical act is simply choosing not to participate in your own oppression.

Three men. Three mediums. One message:

Freedom isn't granted. It's created.

The Thread That Ties Them — And Maybe Us

Kerouac created freedom through story. Parker created it through sound. Gandhi created it through disciplined, deliberate action.

Different tools, same instinct: When the world feels rigid, you improvise your way into a new one.

And that brings me to a brief personal note.

Last week, I shared that my own professional world has shifted — suddenly, unexpectedly, and with a 60-day countdown attached. The response from so many of you was humbling. Thank you to everyone who read, reached out, connected, or simply sent a kind word. It meant more than you know.

In a strange way, these three March 12 giants feel like the right company to keep during a moment of transition. They remind me — and maybe remind you — that reinvention isn't always a choice; sometimes it's a summons.

The Aloha Takeaway

So on this Aloha Thursday, bumped up a day but right on time, I'll leave you with this:

What form in your life is asking to be broken? What march are you quietly beginning? What page are you finally ready to write? What solo are you brave enough to take?

The universe doesn't always align dates for us. But when it does, it's worth listening...and I am all ears. Now's The Time!
